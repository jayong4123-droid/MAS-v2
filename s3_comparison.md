# Swiss vs UK dormant-assets regimes: comparison (every claim quote-verified)

## What the two documents are (read this before comparing)
- **Swiss:** A December 2015 factsheet from the Swiss Bankers Association (an industry body) summarising the amended Banking Act, revised Banking Ordinance and SBA guidelines in force from 1 January 2015. _[SWISS p.2]_ — "The amended Swiss Banking Act and the revised Banking Ordinance entered into force on 1 January 2015, as did the new guidelines of the Swiss Bankers Association" ✔
- **UK:** A March 2014 HM Treasury review of the Dormant Bank and Building Society Accounts Act 2008, laid before Parliament as the Act requires; it evaluates how the scheme has worked rather than restating the rules in full. _[UK p.3]_ — "Presented to Parliament pursuant to section 14(4) of the Dormant Bank and Building Society Accounts Act 2008" ✔
- **Difference:** Not like-for-like: the Swiss text is a banker-facing rules summary, the UK text is a government performance review. Differences below are therefore of the schemes as described, and silence in one document is not evidence the rule does not exist.

## When an account counts as dormant
- **Swiss:** Two stages: 10 years 'without contact', then a further 50 years 'dormant' (60 years after last contact in total). _[SWISS p.2]_ — "after this period, these assets will be deemed "dormant" for a further 50 years" ✔
- **UK:** A single test: no customer-initiated activity for more than 15 years. _[UK p.7]_ — "have not had any customer-initiated activity for more than 15 years" ✔
- **Difference:** UK threshold is 15 years; Swiss assets only reach the end of the process 60 years after last contact.

## Who participates
- **Swiss:** Rules are framed as applying to assets held at Swiss banks generally (the guidelines are about 'assets ... held at Swiss banks'); the document describes no opt-in. _[SWISS p.4]_ — "treatment of assets without contact and dormant assets held at Swiss banks" ✔
- **UK:** Voluntary: banks and building societies must opt in. Ten institutions participate, holding over 90 per cent of UK personal deposits. _[UK p.9]_ — "institutions holding over 90 per cent of personal deposits in the UK are voluntarily utilising the legislation" ✔
- **Difference:** UK is opt-in (with a main and an alternative scheme); the Swiss document presents obligations on banks without any opt-in mechanism.

## Where unclaimed money ends up
- **Swiss:** After publication with no valid claim, the bank transfers the assets to the Federal Department of Finance (the federal government). _[SWISS p.4]_ — "transfer the assets to the Federal Department of Finance" ✔
- **UK:** Banks transfer to the Reclaim Fund Ltd, a non-profit that manages the money, meets reclaims and passes surplus to the Big Lottery Fund for community reinvestment. _[UK p.7]_ — "passing on surplus money for reinvestment in the community through the Big Lottery Fund" ✔
- **Difference:** Swiss: to the state. UK: to an independent non-profit reclaim fund, with surplus directed to good causes.

## Can the owner still claim after the transfer?
- **Swiss:** No. Transfer extinguishes all claims. _[SWISS p.2]_ — "The transfer of the assets renders all claims null and void." ✔
- **UK:** Yes. Money can be reclaimed at any time by those entitled; reclaims are kept separate from the fund's surplus payments to the Big Lottery Fund. _[UK p.11]_ — "dormant account money can be reclaimed at any time by those entitled to the account" ✔
- **Difference:** The sharpest policy contrast: Swiss transfer is final; UK transfer is reversible for the owner.

## Publication and tracing
- **Swiss:** Assets over CHF 500 (or of unknown value) are published on dormantaccounts.ch 60 years after last contact, for one year (five if last contact was 1954 or earlier). Assets up to CHF 500 are transferred after 60 years without being published. Assets above CHF 500 and all safe-deposit boxes are reported to a central database that only the Swiss Banking Ombudsman can search. _[SWISS p.2]_ — "assets up to a maximum value of CHF 500 will be transferred after 60 years without such publication being made" ✔
- **UK:** 'Mylostaccount' is a free tracing service where depositors complete one form to search most banks and building societies and NS&I; BBA, BSA and NS&I run feeder tracing services, and people can also approach institutions directly. _[UK p.11]_ — "a free tracing service which offers a way for depositors to search for their dormant accounts" ✔
- **Difference:** Swiss: institution-led publication late in the cycle plus an Ombudsman-run search. UK: an open, always-on tracing service that also covers accounts dormant for under 15 years.

## Treatment of assets and costs while dormant
- **Swiss:** Rights stay intact until delivery to the federal government; savings keep accruing interest, current-account balances are invested conservatively, and the bank's usual fees continue and it may charge tracing and publication costs to the account. _[SWISS p.6]_ — "The bank's usual costs and fees continue to apply in cases where contact is lost or assets become dormant." ✔
- **Uk:** _not addressed in this document_
- **Difference:** Not addressed in the UK document, so no comparison can be made.

## Scale and evidence of outcomes
- **Swiss:** _not addressed in this document_
- **UK:** About £600 million transferred to the Reclaim Fund Ltd by October 2013; over £180 million passed to the Big Lottery Fund by end-2013; an estimated £200 million reunited with depositors via mylostaccount in six years. _[UK p.10]_ — "By October 2013, £600 million had been transferred by scheme members" ✔
- **Difference:** The Swiss factsheet gives no figures, so the schemes' effectiveness cannot be compared quantitatively from these two documents.

## Features that exist in only one document
- **Swiss:** Safe-deposit boxes are covered: they may be opened where rent is unpaid, and contents stored centrally. _[SWISS p.6]_ — "safe-deposit boxes may be opened in cases where the rent is no longer covered" ✔
- **UK:** An 'alternative scheme' lets smaller institutions split money between the reclaim fund and local charities of their choice; it has no participants at present. _[UK p.10]_ — "The alternative scheme does not have any participants at present." ✔
- **Difference:** Each document describes a mechanism the other does not mention.
